In my current role WHO-i combine population survey and geospatial data to evaluate onchocerciasis mass drug administration, identify geographic gaps and support targeted follow-up and resource allocation.

What would you bring that another analyst may not?

I bring the combination of technical analytics and an evaluation mindset. I can work with complex linked and administrative data, but I also think about study design, equity, governance, interpretation and how findings will change a program decision. I have also worked across university, public-health and research environments, so I am comfortable translating between technical and operational audiences.


Question: This is a 24-month contract and a move from global-health epidemiology into a Canadian healthcare setting. How does this role fit your plans, and why are you interested?

I see this role as a natural next step in my career: applying my epidemiology and data-analysis experience to program evaluation and performance measurement in a Canadian healthcare setting. Across my work at BCCDC and in my current WHO-supported role in Malawi, I’ve analysed complex health and population data, identified gaps and translated findings into practical insights for decision-makers.

I’m especially interested in Touchstone Institute because the Practice Ready Ontario program has a clear, meaningful goal: supporting internationally trained physicians as they transition into practice in Ontario communities. I’d be motivated to help the team understand how the program is performing, strengthen its measurement and data collection, and use evidence to guide ongoing improvement.

The role also brings together the parts of my work I most enjoy: analytical problem-solving, evaluation, collaboration with stakeholders and communicating findings in a way that supports action. The 24-month term feels like a valuable opportunity to make a sustained contribution to the program while continuing to grow as a data and evaluation leader. My experience working across different health and research settings would also help me bring a thoughtful, context-sensitive perspective to the work.
