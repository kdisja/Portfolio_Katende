TOUCHSTONE INSTITUTE INTERVIEW PRACTICE
Manager, Data and Evaluation
Part 4 — Role Fit, Equity, and Closing Questions

QUESTION 12: What's your familiarity with medical education, competency assessment, and licensure?

"I'll be precise about what I don't know. I'm not trained in psychometrics — I'm not going to design a standard-setting exercise or run a DIF analysis myself. What I do know is what those things are for, which questions they answer, and what a credible result looks like, so I can specify, commission, and challenge that work rather than accept it at face value.

I'd come up to speed three ways. Read the foundational material — validity frameworks, CanMEDS, how workplace-based assessment differs from written assessment. Sit with the people doing the work, because the fastest way to understand a measurement system is to watch it operate. And be explicit with the team about what I'm learning, so nobody assumes I know more than I do.

What I bring from day one is the governance and reporting discipline — the definitions, the data quality, the ability to say 'this number is solid' or 'this is indicative only.' That's usually where pathway programs lose credibility, and it's where I'm strongest." ---

QUESTION 13: How do you embed equity and fairness in data and evaluation?

"Equity starts with what we measure and who we ask — and it usually shows up in the disaggregation, not the headline.

In the carcinogen exposures study I integrated the Labour Force Survey with CAREX Canada data to build an exposures-per-worker metric, and the inequities only became visible when we disaggregated by age and immigration status — young workers and recent immigrants carried substantially higher exposures. The aggregate number would have hidden that entirely.

In the NTD evaluation the equity question was geographic: aggregate coverage looked acceptable, but the spatial analysis showed persistent under-coverage concentrated in specific communities. Same principle — the average was hiding who was being missed. That's what the dashboard was built to surface.

My habits are: disaggregate where the sample allows, and be explicit when it doesn't; involve affected groups in design rather than interpreting them afterwards; and watch for indicators that penalise people for access rather than ability — a live risk in an assessment context.

And in reporting I avoid deficit framing. The finding isn't 'this group underperforms' — it's 'this pathway isn't working as well for this group, and here's the mechanism.' That distinction changes what recommendation follows." ---

QUESTION 14: Why Touchstone, and what questions do you have for us?

To close, choose three questions to ask the interview panel.

Possible questions to ask the interview panel (choose three):

1. PRO is in a pilot phase evaluating program design and implementation. Is this role primarily building the evaluation infrastructure for that pilot, or is the pilot already generating data that needs analysis?

2. Where does pathway data live today — the Clinical Field Assessment, placement, and return-of-service tracking? Are they linked, or stitched together manually?

3. Is **rural retention** central to what the funder is asking, versus assessment validity and fairness? I ask because those pull the evaluation in quite different directions.

4. What would 'follow-through' look like in year one — which decisions do you most want this role to influence?

5. How large is the Data & Evaluation team today, and what's the mix of analytical versus coordination work?
